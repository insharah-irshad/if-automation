# IF Automation — Full Theme Details

## 1. Core Theme

**Theme name:** Signal Workshop

**Design direction:** Editorial / industrial / technical hybrid.

IF Automation should feel like a small, precise studio that understands how work moves through a business. The visual language combines the clarity of an editorial publication with the functional character of a technical workshop: warm paper, ink-like typography, orange signal markers, diagrams, handoff notes, and restrained interface motion.

The brand is not positioned as a generic software company or a loud AI start-up. It communicates practical intelligence, human oversight, and systems that remove repetitive work without removing accountability.

> **Brand essence:** Practical AI automation for businesses that want less busywork and more forward motion.

**Personality:** Precise, human, quietly confident.

## 2. Color System

| Role | Color | Hex | Usage |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Paper | Warm paper | `#F4F0E8` | Main page background, cards, navigation surfaces |
| Ink | Deep ink | `#171715` | Main headings, body contrast, borders, buttons |
| Signal Orange | Signal orange | `#F05A3C` | Primary accent, active states, highlights, dots, CTA emphasis |
| Soft Ink | Muted ink | `#686760` | Supporting paragraphs, secondary labels, metadata |
| Rule Gray | Warm rule | `#D8D2C6` | Hairline borders, separators, card outlines |
| White | Clean white | `#FFFFFF` | Small contrast surfaces and circular favicon background |

### Color philosophy

The warm paper background makes the website feel tactile rather than clinical. Ink gives the interface authority and readability. Signal Orange acts like a live indicator on a technical diagram: it draws attention only where action or movement is important.

Orange should remain a controlled signal, not a full-page fill. It is strongest when used for active navigation pills, key words in headlines, CTA emphasis, notification dots, animation states, and selected interface elements.

Avoid purple gradients, excessive neon glow, cold blue dashboards, and large areas of saturated orange. The emotional goal is **calm confidence with visible momentum**.

## 3. Typography System

### Primary display type

**Space Grotesk** is used for the large, direct, high-impact sans-serif headlines and interface labels. It gives the brand a modern technical structure without feeling like a generic SaaS template.

Use it for:

- Hero headline lines.
- Section titles.
- Navigation labels.
- Buttons and CTA text.
- Service titles.
- Numbers and compact interface labels.

### Accent display type

**Instrument Serif** is used for rotating hero words and selected editorial emphasis. Its italic, expressive shape creates contrast against the rigid sans-serif headline and gives the brand a human editorial voice.

Use it for:

- Rotating hero words such as “GROWTH”, “SALES”, “LEADS”, “BIG DECISIONS”, and “THE REST”.
- Carefully selected pull quotes.
- Occasional editorial emphasis.

### Supporting type

**IBM Plex Mono** or a similar monospace treatment is used for small technical labels, counters, section numbers, status text, and compact metadata.

Use it sparingly. Small labels should support the content hierarchy, not compete with the main message.

### Type hierarchy

| Level | Treatment | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Hero heading | Large, heavy Space Grotesk, tight line-height | Immediate positioning statement |
| Rotating word | Bold/italic Instrument Serif, Signal Orange | Human emphasis and movement |
| Section heading | Bold Space Grotesk, strong scale | Section-level promise |
| Body copy | Neutral sans-serif, relaxed line-height | Explain value clearly |
| Navigation | Uppercase or compact Space Grotesk | Fast orientation |
| Micro-label | IBM Plex Mono, uppercase, letter-spaced | Technical context and indexing |
| CTA | Heavy compact sans-serif | Clear action and conversion |

### Typography rules

Headlines should be short, direct, and easy to scan. Paragraphs should not become dense walls of text. The contrast between Space Grotesk and Instrument Serif is a signature feature; it should be preserved rather than replaced with one uniform font.

## 4. Layout Philosophy

The layout follows an **asymmetric editorial canvas** rather than a standard centered marketing grid.

The page uses:

- Strong left-aligned content anchors.
- Large negative space around important statements.
- Editorial section numbers and narrow labels.
- Split compositions where copy and visual systems sit in tension.
- Horizontal flows for process content.
- Full-width signal bands between major sections.
- Deliberate borders and offset layers rather than generic shadows.

The hero uses a text-led left column and an interactive chat visual on the right. The service catalog is structured as a filterable index. The process section moves horizontally from conversation to growth. The proof section uses a central automation network. The team and contact sections become more human and direct as the visitor moves toward conversion.

## 5. Signature Visual Elements

### Signal markers

Small orange numbers, dots, rules, and active states behave like markers on a system map. They indicate where the visitor is in the story or where an interaction is available.

### Workshop cards

Cards resemble clipped technical sheets or working documents. They use thin borders, warm surfaces, small labels, and slightly offset layers. They should feel assembled and purposeful rather than like generic rounded SaaS cards.

### Handoff language

The visual language repeatedly references systems, routing, triggers, thinking, handoffs, replies, and repeatable actions. These words support the core promise: automation makes the path between a customer need and a useful next step more reliable.

### Orange signal band

The full-width orange marquee works as a visual reset between sections. Its continuous movement reinforces the idea of work moving through a system. The animation should remain linear, calm, and readable.

## 6. Navigation Theme

The navigation uses a morphic glass shell: a light, softly bordered container over the warm paper surface, with a dark active pill for the current section.

The navbar includes:

- Home.
- Services.
- How It Works.
- Selected Work.
- Team.
- Contact.
- A separate MAP A WORKFLOW CTA.

The Home item is active at the hero/top position. Navigation links use smooth scrolling. The navbar remains visible at the top, hides when the user scrolls down, and returns after even a small upward scroll. On mobile, it becomes a compact menu with touch-friendly controls.

The navigation should always retain strong contrast. Active items use Ink with Paper text, while the outer shell stays quiet and translucent.

## 7. Hero Section

### Main message

The hero positions the business as the human side of a more automated operation:

> **LET AI HANDLE THE CHATS. YOU HANDLE THE GROWTH.**

The rotating word is the only expressive serif element in the main headline. It changes every few seconds with a restrained fade/slide transition and uses Signal Orange.

### Hero visual

The right side contains a narrow interactive chat artifact. It visualizes a real automation conversation rather than using a generic stock illustration. The visual includes:

- Customer messages.
- An orange IF response.
- A follow-up customer message.
- A thinking indicator.
- A replay control.

Chat bubbles appear sequentially, respond subtly to hover, and can be replayed. The visual should feel like a working system, not a decorative mockup.

### Hero CTAs

- **CONNECT NOW:** Directly opens WhatsApp with the pre-filled message: “Hi! I'd like to learn more about what you offer and how it could help my business.”
- **SEE THE SYSTEMS:** Smooth-scrolls to the work/proof content.

## 8. Service Catalog

The service section uses the headline:

> **WORK LESS. AUTOMATE MORE.**

It presents two main filters:

- ALL SERVICES.
- AI AUTOMATION.
- WEBSITE DEVELOPMENT.

Service cards should remain highly scannable. Each card has a number, a clear service title, a concise description, and a relevant minimalist icon. The visual emphasis comes from spacing, typography, borders, active states, and hover motion rather than excessive decoration.

The cards are responsive. Desktop uses a four-column rhythm where space allows; mobile uses a compact two-column arrangement when readability is preserved. Filter tabs must fit inside the mobile viewport without horizontal scrolling.

The service tone is practical and outcome-led. Examples include AI Agents, Instagram DM Automation, AI Customer Support, Lead Generation & Qualification, Automated Follow-Ups, Appointment & Booking Automation, Email Automation, CRM & Google Sheets Automation, Document & Data Processing, Custom n8n Business Workflows, AI-Powered Reporting, Professional Website Development, E-commerce Websites, Landing Page Design, and Website Redesigns.

## 9. Process Section

The process is horizontal rather than a vertical grid:

1. **WE TALK** — Understand where time is leaking.
2. **WE DESIGN** — Map a system around the real workflow.
3. **WE BUILD** — Connect, test, and launch the automation.
4. **YOU GROW** — Tune, support, and scale the system.

Each step appears as a compact card with a distinct icon and a directional relationship to the next step. Cards reveal sequentially with a restrained pop-up animation. On mobile, the flow remains touch-friendly and can be explored horizontally without cutting off content.

The process strip uses the label **IF / AUTOMATION STEPS**. The old “STATUS: READY TO MAP” label is intentionally not used.

## 10. Proof / Automation Network

The proof section uses a central AI Agent node connected to surrounding workflow nodes. The visual message is that IF Automation connects the tools a business already uses into one reliable operating system.

The network can reference:

- WhatsApp.
- Instagram.
- Email.
- CRM.
- Calendar.
- Data or Sheets.

The animation is fluid but controlled. Hovering or tapping the network replays the reveal. The central node receives a subtle pulse while the animation is active. The animation must not interfere with content reading and should be disabled or simplified for reduced-motion users.

## 16. Motion and Interaction Principles

Motion should communicate system behavior, not decoration.

Use:

- Smooth section scrolling.
- Sequential chat bubble reveals.
- Replay controls for hero and proof visuals.
- Direction-aware navbar visibility.
- Staggered service and process entrances.
- Small hover lifts or color shifts.
- Marquee movement for the orange signal band.
- Paired focus transitions in the team section.
- Accordion expansion on mobile.

Motion rules:

- Prefer transform and opacity for performance.
- Keep interface transitions short and interruptible.
- Use smooth ease-out for entrances and a slightly elastic feel only for larger system-like reveals.
- Do not animate every element at once.
- Keep interaction feedback immediate.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` by removing non-essential movement and replacing it with instant state changes.

## 17. Button and Link Treatment

Primary buttons use Ink backgrounds with Paper text, thin borders, compact uppercase labels, and an arrow or directional indicator. Hover states can shift toward Signal Orange or add a restrained translation of the arrow.

Secondary links use underlines or thin rules rather than large outlined pills. This keeps the page editorial and prevents every action from looking like a button.

WhatsApp actions should remain visually prominent because they are the main conversion path. They should be clearly labeled rather than represented only by an icon.

## 18. Responsive Behavior

### Desktop

Desktop uses the full asymmetric editorial composition: large headline, chat visual, horizontal process, multi-column services, and multi-column footer.

### Mobile

Mobile uses a compact single-column reading flow with selective two-column areas for services and footer content. The primary priorities are:

- No clipped text.
- No unintended horizontal scrolling.
- Touch-friendly buttons and menu controls.
- Hero headline remains readable within the viewport.
- Chat visual becomes narrower without losing the message hierarchy.
- Process cards remain explorable.
- Team profiles become compact expandable rows.
- Contact CTA appears directly under the heading.

## 19. Accessibility and Usability

The design should preserve:

- Strong Ink-on-Paper contrast.
- Visible focus states.
- Descriptive alt text for meaningful imagery.
- Keyboard access to navigation and interactive cards.
- Appropriate semantic headings.
- Functional external links with safe new-tab behavior where appropriate.
- Reduced-motion support.
- Clear WhatsApp labels instead of icon-only actions.

Decorative labels should never be allowed to reduce the readability of the main content. When in doubt, remove metadata before shrinking the actual content.

## 20. Brand Voice

The voice is direct, human, practical, and slightly editorial. It should sound like a smart builder explaining a useful system to a busy business owner.

### Headlines

Use short statements with a point of view:

- “Let AI handle the chats. You handle the growth.”
- “Work less. Automate more.”
- “Four steps. Zero chaos.”
- “Built different. On purpose.”

### CTAs

Use active, specific language:

- “Connect now.”
- “Chat on WhatsApp.”
- “Map a workflow.”
- “See the systems.”

### Microcopy

Keep it clear and grounded:

- “Every good lead gets a useful reply on time.”
- “Your tools, your data.”
- “No lead goes cold.”
- “The repeatable part disappears.”

Avoid generic filler such as “Welcome to our website,” “Innovative solutions for the future,” or “Take your business to the next level.”

## 21. Do / Don’t Summary

| Do | Don’t |
|---|---|
| Use warm paper, ink, and controlled orange | Use purple gradients or generic SaaS blue |
| Keep the layout asymmetrical and editorial | Center every section in the same container |
| Use serif only for deliberate emphasis | Mix many decorative fonts |
| Make automation feel practical and human | Present AI as vague magic |
| Use thin rules and purposeful borders | Put a heavy rounded border around every element |
| Let orange mark action and movement | Fill the entire page with orange |
| Keep cards concise and scannable | Overload cards with metadata |
| Make WhatsApp the easiest conversion path | Reintroduce a long form or dropdown |
| Respect reduced motion | Make every element continuously animate |

## 22. One-Line Design Test

Before adding a new visual treatment, ask:

> **Does this choice reinforce the Signal Workshop idea of precise, human-controlled systems — or does it make IF Automation look like a generic template?**
